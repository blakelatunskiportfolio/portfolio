Case 01

AAA Game Production Breakdown

A hypothetical production plan modeled on God of War (2018) — mapping each phase to what a producer actually owns and manages.

AAA Console Sony Santa Monica ~4 Year Dev Cycle ~200 Developers
Concept
~3 months

Concept Phase

The team — led by creative director Cory Barlog — decides to reboot Kratos into Norse mythology, shifting from hack-and-slash to an intimate father-son story. Core experience pillars are defined: single continuous camera shot, Kratos as a father, Norse world.

Producer manages
  • Pitching timeline to Sony Publishing — securing greenlight and initial budget
  • Early headcount planning: who do we need in the room on day one?
  • Facilitating creative alignment sessions; documenting decisions
  • Setting up foundational tooling (source control, project management, communication channels)
  • Identifying long-lead risks early: single-shot camera is unprecedented at scale
Pre-production
~6–9 months

Pre-Production

Core systems are designed on paper. Art direction is locked. Technology choices are validated. The team grows from ~30 to ~80 people. Key pillars like the Leviathan Axe, Norse realm structure, and Atreus's AI companion role are prototyped as proofs-of-concept.

Producer manages
  • Hiring plan execution — partnering with HR to staff up rapidly
  • Master production schedule draft: mapping all disciplines against milestones
  • Establishing Agile sprint cadence: 2-week sprints, weekly stakeholder standups
  • Budget tracking and headcount forecasting with finance
  • Setting milestone definitions: what does "pre-production done" actually mean?
  • Vendor sourcing for outsourced art and QA pipelines
Vertical Slice
~3–4 months

Vertical Slice

A polished, shippable-quality slice of the game — typically 20–30 minutes. For God of War, this would be the opening sequence: Kratos felling the tree, the Norse village, and the first combat encounter. The slice proves the game's vision is achievable. It goes to Sony for internal review.

Producer manages
  • Scope-locking the slice: ruthlessly cutting anything not essential to prove the vision
  • Coordinating all-hands "slice sprints" with focused, cross-discipline teams
  • Managing Sony Publishing review process: build delivery, feedback loops, turnaround
  • Running postmortem on the slice: what took longer than estimated and why?
  • Updating master schedule based on actual velocity data from slice development
Production
~18–24 months

Full Production

The bulk of development. All nine Norse realms are built in parallel across discipline pods. Content pipelines are running at full capacity — level art, enemy design, narrative scripting, audio, and cinematics all happening simultaneously. Team peaks at ~180–200 people including outsource partners.

Producer manages
  • Running daily standups, weekly leads sync, and monthly milestone reviews
  • Dependency tracking: Atreus animations can't be finalized until gameplay finishes tuning bow mechanics
  • Managing outsource relationships: art vendors, co-development partners for QA
  • Change-control process: creative wants to add a sixth realm — assessing schedule and budget impact
  • Team health monitoring: watching for overload signals, managing sustainable pace
  • Regular scope reviews: what can be cut, deferred to DLC, or shipped as a patch?
Alpha
~3 months

Alpha

The game is playable from beginning to end with all core content in — but unpolished. Performance is inconsistent. Bugs are plentiful. Internal playtests ramp up. Feature freeze kicks in: no new features, only bug fixing and polish from here.

Producer manages
  • Enforcing feature freeze — fielding and declining late creative requests with data
  • QA pipeline scale-up: coordinating internal and external QA teams
  • Bug triage meetings: P1/P2/P3 classification, ownership assignment, daily burn-down
  • Playtest scheduling and feedback collection — routing insights to correct disciplines
  • Preparing Alpha milestone deliverable for Sony Publishing review
Beta
~6–8 weeks

Beta

Game is content-complete and polished. Focus shifts entirely to stability, performance optimization, and localization verification. First-party submission preparation begins. External press embargo content is assembled.

Producer manages
  • Daily P1/P2 bug counts — tracking burn-down against launch date
  • Localization QA coordination (12 languages for God of War)
  • Performance target sign-off: hitting 30fps lock on base PS4 and PS4 Pro targets
  • Press kit and review asset delivery to marketing/communications team
  • Launch readiness checklist: every department signs off on their criteria
Certification
~4–6 weeks

Certification (Cert)

The game is submitted to Sony (TRC — Technical Requirements Checklist) for platform certification. A cert failure can cost 2–4 weeks. Rating submission (ESRB/PEGI) happens simultaneously. Manufacturing masters are prepared for physical distribution.

Producer manages
  • Submitting build to Sony certification team with full TRC documentation
  • Managing cert failure response: fast-tracking fixes, coordinating resubmission
  • ESRB/PEGI rating management — ensuring rating content is accurate and approved
  • Go/no-go decision communication to executive stakeholders
  • Day-0 patch planning: what fixes didn't make cert build that we ship day one?
Launch
Week of April 20, 2018

Launch

God of War ships globally. Review embargo lifts. The team is on call monitoring Metacritic, community forums, and crash telemetry. A launch war room coordinates real-time response to critical issues across engineering, QA, and customer support.

Producer manages
  • Launch war room: 24-hour monitoring of live telemetry, crash reports, community feedback
  • Hot-fix prioritization: what breaks the experience for most players?
  • Communication to Sony Publishing on launch metrics and status
  • Team recognition: acknowledging the effort that got the game across the line
Post-Launch
Ongoing

Post-Launch & Live Ops

Patches, performance updates, New Game+ mode addition, and preparation for God of War: Ragnarök. The team conducts a full project postmortem. Lessons learned feed directly into the next title's production planning.

Producer manages
  • Post-launch patch cadence: routing player feedback into prioritized fix releases
  • Managing team transition: who moves to next project vs. stays for post-launch?
  • Full project postmortem: what went well, what to improve, what to stop doing
  • Seeding the sequel's production plan with this project's learnings

Case 03

Vertical Slice Production Plan

A full production package for a 15-minute stealth mission feature — modeled on an Assassin's Creed-style action-stealth game. The kind of document a producer owns and drives.

Lead Producer 25 Developers Stealth Action Game 12-Week Plan

🗂 Feature Brief

Feature: 15-Minute Stealth Infiltration Mission — "The Merchant's Vault"

Experience goal: Player feels like a ghost — stalking guards, using environmental hazards, and escaping without triggering full alert. The mission validates our core stealth loop before full production.

Success metric: 80% of playtesters complete without triggering full alarm; average time: 12–18 minutes.

Stakeholder: Creative Director sign-off required at Greybox and Vertical Slice gate.

📐 Scope

  • 1 open-plan level (merchant district + vault interior)
  • 1 new enemy archetype: Guard Captain with patrol AI
  • 3 environmental kills (chandelier, barrel, trapdoor)
  • Stealth detection system: sight cones, sound propagation
  • 2 narrative VO scenes (entry + extraction)
  • Greybox → Whitebox → Final Art pass
  • No new UI — reuse existing HUD systems
  • Out of scope: Co-op, multiplayer, new weapons

✅ Definition of Done

Gameplay

  • Stealth mission completable start to finish
  • All three environmental kills functional
  • Guard Captain AI passes all QA test cases
  • Detection system: zero false positives in 20-run regression
  • Zero P1 bugs. Fewer than 5 P2 bugs.

Art & Audio

  • Final lighting pass approved by Art Director
  • All VO recorded and implemented
  • Audio mix signed off by Audio Director
  • No missing textures or geometry seams in final build

Performance

  • Stable 30fps on base console hardware
  • No hitches >2 frames during gameplay
  • Load time under 8 seconds

Publishing

  • Creative Director final sign-off
  • Playtest success rate ≥80%
  • Build submitted to Publishing on schedule
  • Launch criteria doc signed by all leads

🔧 Bug Triage & Change Control

Bug Triage Process

1
Daily QA submission: Bugs logged in Jira by 10am with repro steps, severity, and build version.
2
Triage meeting (30 min, daily): Producer + QA Lead + discipline leads classify bugs: P1 (crash/blocker), P2 (breaks feature), P3 (polish), P4 (nice-to-have).
3
Assignment: P1s assigned immediately, due EOD. P2s assigned same day, due within sprint. P3/P4s queued for polish sprint.
4
Burn-down tracking: P1 and P2 counts tracked daily on the sprint dashboard. Escalate to leads if P1 count doesn't hit zero by content lock.
5
Fix verification: QA verifies all fixes in next available build within 24 hours of fix submission.

Change Control Process

1
Change request submitted: Any scope change requires a written Change Request (CR) — what, why, and who's asking.
2
Impact assessment: Producer evaluates schedule, budget, and dependency impact within 48 hours.
3
Trade required: Every addition must be offset by a cut of equal scope. "Yes, and" only if there's headroom; otherwise "Yes, but we cut X."
4
Approval: CRs above 3 story points require Creative Director + Producer sign-off. All approved CRs are documented and communicated to the team.
5
Post-lock: After content lock (Wk 10), all CRs are automatically deferred to a post-launch patch unless they are P1 bugs.
